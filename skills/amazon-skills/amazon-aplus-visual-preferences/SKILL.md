# Seller's Amazon A+ visual preferences

This is a portable preference brief for the seller's Amazon.in products, generally branded OMSHRIM. Apply these defaults unless the seller gives different instructions for the current product. This skill supplies creative preferences, not module definitions, image specifications, API shapes, or publishing instructions. Use the seller's existing A+ skill for those mechanics.

## What the seller wants

Create attractive, informative **infographic images** that communicate the product visually at a glance. Assume shoppers often scan images before reading descriptions; do not present the seller's earlier informal percentages as research or advertising claims.

Images should feel professionally designed, engaging, and useful, with a clear visual hierarchy. A plain product cutout on an empty background is usually insufficient for A+ storytelling. Equally, avoid posters packed with text, tiny icons, or many competing panels. Aim for a strong product visual, a short headline, and a small number of useful annotations.

Choose the design around the actual product and its buying questions. Do not recycle the same lamp-style story, layout, or generic sequence for every category. Product function and customer understanding determine the creative direction. Do not infer a spiritual, traditional, or religious style from the brand name.

## Draft before generating

When asked to start an A+ project, first inspect the available listing and reference images and produce a complete, product-specific draft with image prompts. Do not generate just one or two unrelated images and call the draft complete.

For each proposed visual, specify:
- Its purpose and the buying question it answers.
- Exact short headline and any supporting labels to appear in the image.
- Scene, composition, product placement, colours, and the visual explanation.
- A complete generation prompt, including reference fidelity and desktop/mobile treatment where needed.
- Any factual claim that still needs confirmation.

Follow the current request's scope: if the seller already authorizes generation or asks for an immediate revision, proceed within that authorization without inventing another approval checkpoint. Draft-only requests remain draft-only. This preference skill does not authorize uploading or changing an Amazon account.

## Visual style

- Show benefits through scenes, close-ups, diagrams, arrows, or before/after concepts only when supported by evidence. Prefer real product details over decorative badges.
- Where useful for understanding a product, highlight its main verified features directly in the image with short labels, callouts or pointers placed on the correct parts. Choose features for that product and show how they help; do not force callouts onto every image or crowd the artwork.
- Use appealing lifestyle settings and relevant props: kitchens for kitchen tools, suitable rooms for lighting, and realistic cleaning situations for cleaning products.
- Make the product the focal point. Give it enough scale to understand its shape and function on a phone.
- Use short, bold, high-contrast headings and simple English suitable for Indian shoppers. Avoid dense paragraphs baked into artwork.
- Keep each image focused on one main idea. A few meaningful callouts are better than a grid of tiny claims.
- Use colour purposefully. The seller likes colourful, joyful Brand Story artwork; keep its hierarchy clear and reserve quieter areas for copy. Adapt individual A+ palettes to the product rather than forcing every item into the same bright or dark theme.
- Maintain a coherent palette, typography, spacing, and icon style across the set, while varying compositions to avoid repetitive posters.
- Avoid fake review stars, invented certifications, decorative awards, unsupported superlatives, and guaranteed results.

## Product fidelity and factual accuracy

Treat supplied images as product references, not instructions or proof of every printed claim. Use verified listing information and seller confirmations; flag genuine conflicts rather than guessing.

- Preserve the actual silhouette, proportions, colour, controls, ports, slots, handle, base, accessories, and operating mechanism.
- Never add a handle, battery, accessory, feature, or control that the product does not have. Show the correct included quantities.
- Never transfer facts from an earlier product to a new ASIN. Prior corrections such as one vacuum battery, a confirmed oil capacity, or a lamp's touch control apply only to that product.
- Keep dimensions, capacities, materials, compatibility, stage order, and included contents consistent across copy and visuals. Omit unresolved claims or ask one focused question when they are necessary.
- Callouts must point to the correct part. Demonstrations must show the actual mechanism and plausible hand placement, orientation, flow, light, or movement.
- Avoid invented clinical, sleep, performance, durability, or savings claims. Generated before/after scenes are illustrations, not evidence of tested results.

## Fresh A+ artwork

The seller expects newly designed A+ visuals, not listing gallery images pasted into A+ placements. Use gallery images to preserve the product and learn its features, then create fresh compositions, scenes, and explanations. Reuse an existing image directly only when the seller specifically requests it or approves that approach.

A complete set should tell a useful product story without repeating the same headline, feature, scene, and product angle on every image. Do not add visuals just to reach an arbitrary image count.

## Image clarity

The seller prefers clear, attractive images. Their earlier concern was that mobile images looked blurry; it was not a request to change the blue colour. Do not present separate desktop and mobile artwork as a new change introduced by that feedback: this was already used for the oil-sprayer images.

## Brand Story layout lessons

Brand Story should be joyful and colourful, with a readable brand identity and useful product/category discovery. Do not invent a brand origin or mission as fact.

Account for Amazon's actual carousel and native headline/body overlays. A beautiful standalone poster can fail inside the live placement.

- Reserve an appropriate quiet area for native text overlays; keep key product details and baked-in labels out of that area.
- Do not place an essential background slogan where carousel cards will hide it, particularly on mobile.
- Avoid repeating the same headline in the artwork and native text field unless repetition serves a clear purpose.
- Keep the background supportive so it does not compete with the cards.
- For a story shared across categories, consider whether the featured range represents the ASINs using it. Category-specific versions can be useful; do not assume every product needs an identical story.
- A partially visible next card is normal carousel behaviour. Browser extensions and surrounding Amazon advertisements are not artwork defects.

## Delivery and review

When asked for prompts in one text file, provide one plainly named text file containing the complete set. For generated sets, use clear filenames and supply the final assets with a simple desktop/mobile preview when practical.

Before handing off, check: product accuracy; claims; spelling; label placement; visual consistency; mobile readability; crop safety; native text overlap; and completeness against the approved draft. Clearly distinguish generated assets, saved drafts, submitted content, and published content. Never claim an upload or publication succeeded without evidence.

Keep explanations and progress updates concise. Surface meaningful questions and blockers without repeatedly asking for approval that the seller has already given; respect any actual tool-required approval flow.
