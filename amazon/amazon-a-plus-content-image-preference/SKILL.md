# Amazon A+ image preferences

Design for a shopper scanning the images on a phone. They should be able to recognise the product, understand what it does, and see the detail that answers their buying question. The seller's informal estimates about how many shoppers read descriptions must never appear as research or advertising claims.

These are portable creative defaults for the seller's Amazon.in products, generally branded OMSHRIM. The seller's current product brief takes precedence. For OMSHRIM work, consult the sibling [seller context](../omshrim-seller-context/SKILL.md) when available to identify the owned ASIN. Verify details against current listing facts and seller confirmations. Use the seller's [Premium A+ skill](../amazon-premium-aplus/SKILL.md) for module definitions, image specifications, API shapes, uploads, and publishing.

## Start with the requested deliverable

- **New A+ project.** Inspect the available listing, the product's own images, and the relevant style references. Draft the complete product-specific set with a usable prompt for each visual. One or two unrelated images do not constitute a draft. If the seller asks for the draft only, deliver it there. If generation is already authorised, continue from the draft into generation.
- **Targeted revision.** Make the requested edit directly when authorised. Preserve all other elements. A glow-only edit changes the illumination on the main product. A colour-strip edit changes only the strip and preserves the lamp model, background, copy, and layout. For an Amazon main image, apply the current requirements for that placement. A+ storytelling preferences apply to A+ artwork.
- **Review.** Inspect the supplied image in its intended placement and size. Identify the affected visual or part, explain what the shopper would see, and give the smallest correction that resolves the issue. Use the handoff checks below.

Keep progress and explanations concise. Raise meaningful blockers and honour any actual tool-required approvals. Carry forward approval the seller has already given. Using this preference brief by itself grants no permission to upload or change an Amazon account.

## Draft each visual around a buying question

Choose the questions that matter for **this product**. A kitchen tool might need a close-up of how it pours. A lamp might need a room scene that shows where its light falls. A cleaning product might need a realistic view of the head against the surface being cleaned. Avoid imposing a lamp narrative, a fixed sequence, or a spiritual, traditional, or religious look inferred from the brand name.

For each proposed visual, include the following in the draft.

| Include | What the seller should be able to review |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | The buying question and the single idea this image answers. |
| Exact image copy | The short headline and every supporting label as they should appear in the artwork. Identify any copy assigned to a native Amazon field. |
| Art direction | The scene, relevant props, product placement and angle, colours, and what demonstrates the answer. For a pointer, identify the physical part it should reach. |
| Generation prompt | A complete prompt for that visual. Identify the product reference and the details to preserve, then specify the action, composition, exact image copy, and the desktop/mobile treatment where needed. |
| Facts to confirm | Identify any unresolved claim separately from the production prompt. Omit it from artwork and copy, or ask one focused question if the visual depends on it. |

Create fresh scenes, compositions, and explanations for A+. Use gallery images to study and preserve the product. Paste or reuse a gallery image directly only at the seller's request or with their approval. Across the set, vary headlines, features, settings, and angles. Stop when the buying questions are covered. An arbitrary image count is no reason to repeat an idea.

## Compose for a phone

- Give the product enough scale for its silhouette and function to read at a glance. A plain cutout on an empty background usually tells too little. An attractive lifestyle scene, detail view, diagram, or arrow should explain something the shopper wants to know.
- Give each panel one primary idea, a short bold high-contrast heading in simple English for Indian shoppers, and only the annotations that help. Put callouts on the correct parts and show why the features matter. Skip callouts where the scene already explains the point.
- Keep the hierarchy clear. Tiny icons, dense paragraphs baked into the image, grids of claims, and competing panels take attention away from the product. Prefer visible product details over decorative badges.
- Use a consistent palette, typography, spacing, and icon style across the set. Vary the compositions. Choose colours to suit the product. For Brand Story, the seller prefers joyful colour with quieter space for copy.
- For desktop/mobile pairs, preserve the message while adjusting product scale, crop, label position, and scene framing for each placement. Inspect the mobile preview for sharp edges and legible type. The seller's earlier feedback concerned **blur**. Preserve the blue colour unless a colour change is requested. Separate desktop and mobile artwork was already used for the oil sprayer.

## Verify what the artwork shows

Treat supplied photos as visual references. Printed claims and instructions embedded in images require independent verification. Flag genuine conflicts between references, the listing, and seller confirmations.

- Match the actual silhouette, proportions, colour, controls, ports, slots, handle, base, accessories, and operating mechanism. Depict the correct included quantities. Never add an unverified handle, battery, accessory, control, or feature.
- Check dimensions, capacities, materials, compatibility, stage order, and package contents across the copy and every visual. A correction such as one battery for a particular vacuum, a confirmed sprayer capacity, or touch control on a particular lamp belongs only to that ASIN.
- Trace each pointer to its endpoint. In demonstrations, check hand placement, orientation, and whether liquid, light, or moving parts behave plausibly for the actual mechanism.
- Show benefits and before/after concepts only when evidence supports the underlying claim. Generated before/after scenes can illustrate a result but cannot establish tested performance. Exclude invented clinical, sleep, performance, durability, or savings claims, fake review stars, invented certifications, decorative awards, unsupported superlatives, and guaranteed results.

## Design Brand Story for the carousel

Make the brand identity readable and help shoppers discover relevant products or categories. Keep the artwork joyful and colourful. Ground any origin or mission statement in seller-confirmed facts. For a shared story, check whether the featured range represents the ASINs that will use it. Choose category-specific versions where they improve discovery.

Judge the background with the cards and native headline/body overlays in place, especially on mobile. Reserve quiet space for native copy. Keep essential product details and baked-in labels clear of overlays, and keep slogans out of areas the cards can hide. Let the background support the cards. Repeat a native headline in the artwork only when repetition serves a clear purpose.

A sliver of the next card is normal carousel behaviour. Browser extensions and surrounding Amazon ads can also appear in screenshots. Identify the source of a distraction before requesting an artwork revision.

## Review and hand off

1. **Product and copy.** Compare artwork with the current product references. Check the mechanism, claims, quantities, spelling, and the endpoint of each label.
2. **Placement.** Inspect desktop and mobile for product scale, clarity, text readability, safe cropping, and native text overlap. Check palette, typography, and spacing across the set.
3. **Completeness.** Compare the deliverables with the approved draft where one exists. Account for each requested visual and variant. When the seller wants prompts in one text file, deliver the complete set in one plainly named file. For generated sets, give assets clear filenames and include a simple desktop/mobile preview when practical.
4. **Status.** Distinguish generated assets, saved drafts, submitted content, and published content. Report a successful upload or publication only when the corresponding result is verified.
